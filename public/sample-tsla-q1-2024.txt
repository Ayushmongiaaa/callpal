TESLA, INC. (NASDAQ: TSLA)
First Quarter 2024 Update Call
April 23, 2024

NOTE: This is a condensed sample transcript prepared for demonstrating CallPal.
It paraphrases publicly reported results and commentary and is not a verbatim
record of the call.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------

OPERATOR: Good afternoon and welcome to Tesla's first quarter 2024 update call.
Joining us are Elon Musk, Chief Executive Officer, and Vaibhav Taneja, Chief
Financial Officer.

VAIBHAV TANEJA (CFO), PREPARED REMARKS:

Total revenue for the first quarter was 21.3 billion dollars, down 9 percent
year over year. Automotive revenue declined 13 percent year over year, driven
by lower average selling prices and lower deliveries.

We delivered 386,810 vehicles in the quarter and produced 433,371, so we built
meaningfully more than we delivered and inventory increased. Several factors
weighed on the quarter: the early ramp of the updated Model 3 at Fremont,
factory shutdowns from shipping diversions in the Red Sea, and the arson
attack that halted production at Gigafactory Berlin.

Operating income was 1.2 billion dollars and operating margin was 5.5 percent,
down year over year. GAAP net income was 1.1 billion dollars. Free cash flow
was negative 2.5 billion dollars in the quarter, reflecting a 2.7 billion
dollar increase in inventory and 1.0 billion dollars of AI infrastructure
capital expenditure.

Energy storage deployments reached a record 4.1 gigawatt hours, and energy
generation and storage gross margin reached a record. Services and Other
revenue grew 25 percent year over year.

On the outlook, we are between two major growth waves. We expect the vehicle
volume growth rate in 2024 to be notably lower than the growth rate achieved
in 2023. We are updating our future vehicle lineup to accelerate the launch of
new models ahead of our previously communicated start of production in the
second half of 2025.

ELON MUSK (CEO), PREPARED REMARKS:

Regarding the product roadmap, we have updated our plan to accelerate the
launch of new models. These will use aspects of the next generation platform
as well as aspects of our current platforms, and will be produced on the same
manufacturing lines as our current lineup. This is more capital efficient than
building entirely new lines.

On autonomy, we recently released a version of Full Self-Driving that is a
significant improvement. We are also in discussions with a major automaker
about licensing FSD.

Energy storage will grow much faster than the automotive business. We think
the stationary storage business is going to be extremely large.

QUESTION AND ANSWER SESSION

ANALYST, DEUTSCHE BANK: Can you clarify the timing on the new models?

ELON MUSK: We expect to begin production of new models in early 2025, possibly
late this year. That is ahead of the prior plan.

ANALYST, WEDBUSH: How should we think about margins from here?

VAIBHAV TANEJA: Pricing remains a challenge. We expect continued pressure on
automotive gross margin in the near term as we balance volume and price. We
are focused on cost reduction per vehicle, which declined again this quarter.

ANALYST, MORGAN STANLEY: What is the capital commitment to AI?

ELON MUSK: We expect capital expenditures to exceed 10 billion dollars this
year, with a substantial portion going to AI compute. We are installing
significant training capacity. That said, I want to be clear that this is a
large spend and it does compress near-term free cash flow.

ANALYST, CANACCORD: Any comment on demand in China?

VAIBHAV TANEJA: The market there is highly competitive and price competition
has intensified. We continue to see pressure on pricing across the region.

OPERATOR: That concludes today's call. Thank you.
