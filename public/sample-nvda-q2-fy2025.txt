Second Quarter Fiscal Year 2025 Earnings Conference Call
August 28, 2024

OPERATOR: Good afternoon and welcome to NVIDIA's second quarter fiscal 2025
earnings call. Joining us are Jensen Huang, President and Chief Executive
Officer, and Colette Kress, Executive Vice President and Chief Financial
Officer.

COLETTE KRESS (CFO), PREPARED REMARKS:

Thank you. We delivered another record quarter. Revenue for the second quarter
was 30.0 billion dollars, up 15 percent sequentially and up 122 percent year
over year, and well above our outlook of 28.0 billion dollars.

Data Center revenue reached a record 26.3 billion dollars, up 16 percent
sequentially and up 154 percent year over year. Growth was driven by strong
demand for the Hopper architecture, and in particular H200, which began
shipping in volume during the quarter. Cloud service providers represented
roughly 45 percent of Data Center revenue, with more than 50 percent coming
from consumer internet and enterprise customers.

Gaming revenue was 2.88 billion dollars, up 9 percent sequentially and up 16
percent year over year. Professional Visualization was 454 million dollars.
Automotive was 346 million dollars, up 37 percent year over year, driven by
autonomous driving platforms.

GAAP gross margin was 75.1 percent and non-GAAP gross margin was 75.7 percent.
Margins declined sequentially, reflecting a higher mix of new products within
Data Center and inventory provisions for low-yielding Blackwell material.

Turning to the outlook. For the third quarter, we expect revenue of 32.5
billion dollars, plus or minus 2 percent. We expect GAAP and non-GAAP gross
margins of 74.4 percent and 75.0 percent respectively. We expect full year
gross margins to be in the mid-70s percent range.

I do want to be direct about a few pressures. We executed a change to the
Blackwell GPU mask to improve production yield, and as a result Blackwell
production is expected to ramp in the fourth quarter. Gross margins are
expected to moderate into the low-70s percent range later in the year as we
ramp new products. Supply for Hopper remains constrained, and we expect demand
to exceed supply into next year.

JENSEN HUANG (CEO), PREPARED REMARKS:

Hopper demand remains strong and the anticipation for Blackwell is incredible.
We shipped customer samples of Blackwell in the quarter. The mask change is
complete and there were no functional changes required.

We are seeing a broad platform shift. General purpose computing is giving way
to accelerated computing, and there is roughly one trillion dollars of
installed data center infrastructure that will be modernized over the coming
years. Generative AI is a second and additive driver on top of that.

Sovereign AI is a new growth vector. We expect sovereign AI revenue in the low
double digit billions this year, up from nothing a year ago, as nations build
domestic compute capacity.

QUESTION AND ANSWER SESSION

ANALYST, GOLDMAN SACHS: Jensen, can you address the Blackwell timing concerns?
There were reports of a delay.

JENSEN HUANG: The change to the mask was to improve yield. Blackwell production
ramps in the fourth quarter and we expect several billion dollars of Blackwell
revenue in Q4. Hopper demand continues through that transition. I would not
characterize this as a delay to the overall ramp.

ANALYST, MORGAN STANLEY: Colette, how should we think about the gross margin
trajectory?

COLETTE KRESS: We expect margins to move to the low-70s as Blackwell ramps,
because new product introductions carry higher cost early in the cycle. We
expect margins to recover as the ramp matures. For the full year we still
expect mid-70s.

ANALYST, BERNSTEIN: Is there a risk that customers pause Hopper purchases while
waiting for Blackwell?

JENSEN HUANG: We are not seeing that. Demand for Hopper exceeds supply today.
Customers need capacity now, and the infrastructure is fungible across both
architectures.

ANALYST, UBS: Any commentary on China?

COLETTE KRESS: Our Data Center revenue in China grew sequentially and is a
significant contributor. However, the market there remains very competitive,
and export controls continue to limit what we can ship. We expect China to
remain competitive going forward.

ANALYST, TD COWEN: How durable is sovereign demand?

JENSEN HUANG: Every country recognizes that their data is a national resource.
We think this becomes a persistent category rather than a one-time build.

OPERATOR: That concludes today's call. Thank you for joining.
